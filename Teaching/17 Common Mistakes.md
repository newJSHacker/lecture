# 17 — Common mistakes

These are the failure modes that look like hard work from the inside.

## Covering instead of teaching

You “got through” clip space, PBR, and shadows. Students can do none of them. Coverage is a plan. Learning is a result.

**Repair:** one idea, one attempt, one check. Cut the third topic.

## Slides as the course

The deck is a 90-page PDF. Lab is “do the tutorial.” You are a projector operator.

**Repair:** board the argument; lab continues the same object; slides hold pictures.

## Demo of a finished cathedral

You open a production repo and click play. Students are impressed and learn nothing they can redo.

**Repair:** start from their starter. Build one slice. See [[Teaching/06 Live Coding Pedagogy]].

## Environment as the exam

Half the lab is NVIDIA drivers, permissions, and `npm`. The geometric idea never appears.

**Repair:** one-command starter, lab image, pre-lab install hour.

## Invisible success condition

“Make something cool with Three.js.” Anxiety, then three.js examples pasted.

**Repair:** a screenshot, a test, a rubric. Cool is the “exceeds” row.

## Feedback after the course is over

Final comments on a project they will never open again.

**Repair:** milestones and a week to act. [[Teaching/08 Feedback and Rubrics]]

## Grading theater

You spend 12 minutes per lab writing essays. You are late. Students do not read them.

**Repair:** rubric + two comments + class-wide pattern.

## Moving the stack every year

WebGL one year, WebGPU the next, a new framework in week 3 because you are bored.

**Repair:** freeze the student-facing stack for the term. You may preview new tech in week 15.

## Teaching only the students who are already good

You riff with the front row. The back row is lost and polite.

**Repair:** look at the starter files, not the faces. Target the middle. Valves for the top.

## Humiliation as standards

Sarcasm, surprise orals for people who fear them, public shame for a black screen.

**Repair:** high standards, private correction, public principles. [[Teaching/10 Inclusive Teaching and Accessibility]]

## Policy you will not keep

“No late work.” Then you accept everything. Students learn that written rules are fiction.

**Repair:** write the rule you will actually run.

## Ignoring AI

A take-home essay is now a weak assessment.

**Repair:** [[Teaching/12 Academic Integrity and AI]]

## Scope creep in projects

Every idea is “a small engine.” Nobody ships.

**Repair:** must/should/may. Feature freeze. [[Teaching/09 Assessment Design]]

## No names, no presence

You do not know who is drowning.

**Repair:** roster, lab circulation, one early email. [[Teaching/11 Mentoring and Office Hours]]

## Heroics

You answer Slack at 01:00, rewrite the lab at 08:00, and resent the room at 09:00.

**Repair:** [[Teaching/15 Time Management for Instructors]]

## Confusing research talks with undergraduate lectures

A seminar on your paper is not Week 3 of Computer Graphics I.

**Repair:** prerequisites, pictures, an attempt. Save the paper for a guest hour with scaffolding.

## Never watching yourself

You have taught five years and have one year of experience five times.

**Repair:** record, observe, change one thing. [[Teaching/16 Teaching Portfolio and Growth]]

## Exercise

Circle the three mistakes you have made. Write one repair you will run in the next two weeks. Tell a colleague so it is real.
