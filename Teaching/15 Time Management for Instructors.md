# 15 — Time management for instructors

Teaching expands to fill every evening if you let it. A good teacher is available enough to diagnose and not so available that they never prepare, rest, or do research.

Burnout looks like irritability in lab, late grades, and cynicism about “this generation.” Prevent it as an operations problem.

## Count the real hours

A 3-credit course is not 3 hours. A honest week:

| Task | Hours (typical) |
| --- | --- |
| Contact (lecture + lab) | 4–6 |
| Prep (first year) | 6–10 |
| Prep (later years, if you reuse) | 2–4 |
| Office hours | 2 |
| Grading (if no TA, 40 students) | 4–8 |
| Email / forum | 1–3 |
| Course improvements | 1–2 |

If the department assigned you three new preps, you must cut something: assessment frequency, project ambition, or research. Do it in writing with your chair. Silent heroism fails in week 10.

## Prep that scales

- Reuse the visualizer and the kernel
- Change one thing per week, not the whole stack
- Record a demo once; live-code the decisions around it
- A comment bank
- A quiz bank
- Freeze the lab on Friday; do not rewrite it at 8:00

The first year is expensive. Year 2 should be cheaper if you kept notes ([[Teaching/03 Lesson Planning]]).

## Grading that scales

- Rubrics with few cells
- Autograde the kernel
- Human-grade the picture and the paragraph
- Return a class-wide pattern in lecture instead of 40 identical essays
- Do not accept “just look at my GitHub” as a submission format

See [[Teaching/08 Feedback and Rubrics]].

## Email rules

- Batch twice a day
- A FAQ page you can paste
- “If this is a grade dispute, use the regrade form”
- You do not owe a same-hour answer on Sunday

Students will adapt to the system you actually run, not the one you wish you had.

## Saying no

You will be asked to: guest-lecture, join a committee, advise extra theses, write a letter this week, add a student after week 3, change a deadline for a whole class because a different course had a midterm.

Use a criterion: does this help **this** course’s learning goals this term? If not, “not this semester” is a complete sentence.

Cap thesis load. A careful supervisor with four students beats a neglectful supervisor with twelve. See [[Teaching/22 Supervising Theses]].

## Calendar hygiene

- Put prep on the calendar as a meeting with yourself
- Put grade-return dates on the student calendar so you are stuck with them
- Protect one half-day with no teaching tasks
- Sleep. A tired live-coder is a rude live-coder

## Perfectionism

A B+ lab that exists on Monday beats an A+ lab that appears Wednesday at noon. Ship the starter.

Your lecture does not need to be a conference talk. It needs a goal, a picture, and an attempt.

## Life

Caregiving, illness, and research deadlines are real. Tell the chair early. Use the backup plan in [[Teaching/13 Classroom Difficulties]]. Students can survive a cancelled lecture. They cannot survive a semester of a disappearing examiner.

## Exercise

Track one week in 30-minute blocks. Circle the hours that did not help a student learn or help you recover. Cut one of those recurring blocks next week.
