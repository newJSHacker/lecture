# 11 — Mentoring and office hours

Office hours are not a second lecture for the students who like you. They are a clinic. Mentoring is the longer relationship: projects, theses, careers, and the student who is about to disappear.

## How to run office hours

**Publish them.** Same place, same time, most weeks. If you cancel, say so early.

**Format options:**

| Format | Best for | Watch-out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open room, queue on the board | Labs, install, short bugs | One student monopolizes |
| 15-minute bookings | Thesis, grades, sensitive topics | Empty slots if you over-book friction |
| Hybrid: first hour drop-in, second hour booked | Mixed courses | You must enforce the split |
| TA hours + your hours | Large courses | Students must know who is for what |

**In the room:**

- Door open if you are alone with one student
- Laptop facing so you both see
- Timer visible for the queue
- “Show me the repo,” not “tell me the story for 10 minutes”

**The 12-minute bug clinic:**

1. Reproduce
2. Hypothesis
3. One experiment
4. They type the fix
5. They say what they will do next

If you type the fix, you feel useful and they learned your keyboard.

## Who does not come

The students who need you most often do not appear. They are ashamed, working a job, or lost enough that they cannot form a question.

Moves:

- After a failed quiz, write a short note: “Come Thursday with the starter; no speech required.”
- Stay 10 minutes after lab
- Watch who never submits; email once, early, with a concrete offer
- Do not rely only on “my door is always open.” That sentence is not a system

## Academic mentoring

Help them choose a project that is **narrow enough to finish**. The amateur mentor inflates scope because the idea is exciting.

A good undergraduate project: one core algorithm or one rendering feature, one evaluation, one honest limitation. See [[Teaching/22 Supervising Theses]] and [[Graduation Requirements]].

Ask every two weeks:

- What runs?
- What is the risk?
- What will you cut?

## Career mentoring (keep it in your lane)

You can:

- Review a portfolio site for clarity and honesty
- Explain what a graphics/web internship looks at (repo, demo, README)
- Introduce them to a guest or alum **with permission**
- Write a letter if you have seen their work

You should not:

- Promise a job
- Push them into your startup as unpaid labor
- Write a letter for someone whose work you have not seen
- Give immigration or legal advice

## Emotional distress

You are not their therapist. You are a mandatory reporter in some systems; know your campus rule.

If a student is in crisis:

- Listen, do not interrogate
- Do not promise total secrecy if you cannot keep it
- Walk them to counseling services if they agree, or give the number and stay until they have a next step
- If there is imminent risk of harm, contact campus emergency services
- Afterward, write the facts (time, what was said, what you did), not a novel
- Take care of yourself; tell a chair if you need backup

Do not diagnose. Do not tell them to “just push through the shader.”

## Power, boundaries, dual relationships

- No dating students in your course or under your supervision
- No drinking with undergraduates you grade
- No private money lending
- Gifts: follow campus policy; when in doubt, refuse kindly
- Social media: do not follow student private accounts; keep a public professional page if you want

Warmth is allowed. Confusion about roles is not.

## Letters of recommendation

Say yes only if you can be specific. Ask for:

- Deadline and form
- CV and unofficial transcript
- Project they want you to mention
- Draft of their statement if they have one

Write about work you saw. “Hard-working and passionate” is a discarded letter.

If you cannot write a strong letter, say so early: “I can confirm you took the course; I cannot write a competitive letter because I did not see project-level work.”

## Mentoring strong students

Do not only spend time on firefighting. Give strong students a harder valve, a paper, a TA tryout, or a tighter critique. Neglecting them is also bad teaching.
