# 20 — Questioning and discussion

The quality of the class is the quality of the questions and the wait after them. A lecture full of rhetorical “right?” is a monologue with extra syllables.

## Wait time

Ask. Then be quiet for **5–7 seconds**. It will feel like a minute. If you answer your own question at second two, you have trained them not to think.

If the room is large, take written answers first so thinking is not public yet.

## A ladder of questions

| Level | Example in IGWT | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recall | “What does `orient > 0` mean in our convention?” | Opening retrieval |
| Describe | “What is on the screen after the vertex shader?” | After a demo |
| Predict | “If I flip the winding, what happens?” | Before you flip it |
| Diagnose | “The mesh is black. Where do you look first?” | Checklist practice |
| Justify | “Why not sort in place here?” | After they have a kernel |
| Transfer | “Where does this show up in picking?” | End of a topic |
| Critique | “Does this README let a stranger run the project?” | Studio |

Do not start a new topic with a critique question. Novices cannot critique a schema they do not have.

## Cold call, warm call

**Warm call:** warn them (“I will ask you to trace this in a minute”). Humane and effective.

**Cold call:** use sparingly, after norms exist, never as punishment.

**Never:** pick on someone who looks lost to make an example of them.

A good pattern: “Write for 45 seconds. Pair. I will ask two pairs.” Then it is not a raid.

## Wrong answers

Wrong answers are data.

> “That would be true if we were in view space. We are still in world space. How would we check?”

Do not say “no” and move to a favorite student. The room learns that only finished thoughts are welcome.

If the answer is unsafe (harassment), that is [[Teaching/13 Classroom Difficulties]], not a Socratic moment.

## Pair and write

Think-pair-share is not a gimmick. It is how you get more than three voices.

- 45–90 seconds silent write
- 2 minutes pair
- Harvest 2–3 answers on the board (names optional)

Use a shared doc in large rooms if speaking is a barrier ([[Teaching/10 Inclusive Teaching and Accessibility]]).

## Discussion that is not a bull session

Give a **text or artifact**: a 15-line kernel, a screenshot, a short paragraph from a paper, a README.

Questions:

1. What is the claim?
2. What would falsify it?
3. What would you change?

Timebox. End with a written takeaway so it does not evaporate.

## Handling “I don’t know”

Praise the honesty. Narrow:

> “Is the problem the sign, the sort, or the visualizer?”

Give a 50/50 if needed. Then they can enter.

## Online / hybrid

Do not ask “questions in chat” and ignore the chat. Assign a TA as chat navigator. Repeat the question aloud for the recording.

## Exercise

Write six questions for your next lecture, one from each ladder row except critique. Put the predict question **before** the demo, not after.
