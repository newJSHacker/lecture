# Teaching handbook — how to use these notes

Parent: [[06 Becoming a Good Teacher]]

These notes are a practical handbook for instructors in **Interactive Graphics and Web Technologies (IGWT)**. They assume you already have the curriculum in [[01 subjects]] and the course skeleton in [[02 Curriculum Design Advice]].

They are not a theory of education degree. They are what a competent colleague would tell you in the week before you teach.

## Who this is for

- A new instructor assigned an IGWT course
- An engineer moving from industry into teaching
- A TA who will run labs or live-coding sessions
- An experienced lecturer who has never taught graphics by building
- A program lead who must coach other teachers

## How to read

**If you have 90 minutes before the semester:** [[Teaching/01 What Good Teaching Looks Like]], [[Teaching/04 First Day and Syllabus]], [[Teaching/19 Checklists and Templates]], [[Teaching/23 First Year as an Instructor]].

**If you have a month:** read the folder in numeric order. Do the exercises at the end of each note. Put artifacts in your teaching portfolio ([[Teaching/16 Teaching Portfolio and Growth]]).

## What “good” means here

A good IGWT teacher produces students who can:

1. Explain a concept without the slide.
2. Implement a small version from a blank file.
3. Debug a broken scene, shader, or predicate.
4. Justify a design choice to a skeptic.
5. Finish a project that another machine can run.

If your lectures are admired and those five things do not happen, the teaching is not yet good.

## House rules for these notes

- Prefer one concrete classroom move over three slogans.
- Prefer IGWT examples (canvas, WebGL, Three.js, GLSL, glTF, XR) over generic “STEM.”
- Prefer checklists you can print.
- Do not invent student data. When a table is a template, it says so.
- Do not submit student work that was generated from these samples as original research.
