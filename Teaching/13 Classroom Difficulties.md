# 13 — Classroom difficulties

Most weeks are ordinary. Some minutes are not. Prepare a few scripts so you do not invent policy under adrenaline.

## Silence

“Any questions?” is not a method. See [[Teaching/20 Questioning and Discussion]].

If the room is silent after a hard idea:

- Give a 60-second write
- Then pair
- Then take two written answers
- Then you talk

Silence after “clone the repo” usually means the repo is broken.

## The student who dominates

Thank them, park it, widen:

> “I want to hear that idea in a minute. I am going to take two other voices first.”

Talk to them privately if it continues: you value their energy; the room needs air. Give them a challenge valve or a TA-style job (note the board), not a license to lecture.

## Side talk and phones

Proximity first. Stand near. If it continues, a calm public line:

> “I need this table on the shader for five minutes.”

Do not humiliate. Do not hold a 10-minute speech about respect; it rewards the disruption.

If a student is on a laptop doing other work, you may set a laptop-down stretch for 8 minutes of board work. Do not run a police state. Design the class so there is something to do with the machine.

## Chronic lateness

Start on time with a retrieval quiz. Late arrival then has a cost that is not a scolding. If someone is late every time because of work or transit, problem-solve once privately.

## Challenges to your expertise

A student says you are wrong. Sometimes they are right.

> “Show me. If you are right, we will all learn it. If not, we will find the mismatch in convention.”

If you were wrong, say so, fix the note, move on. Authority grows.

If it is a performance, timebox: “Office hours. We have a lab to start.”

## Anger, insults, harassment

- Stop the content.
- “That language is not allowed in this room.”
- If it is aimed at a person: separate, document, follow Title IX / campus harassment process as required.
- Do not debate slurs.

If you feel unsafe, end the meeting. Call campus security. You are not required to absorb abuse to be a dedicated teacher.

## Crying, panic, shutdown

Quiet voice. Offer water and a break. Do not make them explain in front of the room. See [[Teaching/11 Mentoring and Office Hours]] for crisis limits.

## Group explosion

A team emails that one member did nothing. Do not adjudicate by vibe.

- Look at commits, issues, and the contribution statement
- Meet them together once, then separately if needed
- Use the written process in the spec
- Split the grade only if the spec allows it
- Document

Prevent this with milestones and individual orals ([[Teaching/09 Assessment Design]]).

## Cheating accusation in public

Do not accuse in the lab. “Please see me after class” and then the process in [[Teaching/12 Academic Integrity and AI]].

## You lost your temper

Stop. Apologize for the tone, not for a standard. Repair in the next meeting if needed. Tell a mentor if you are ashamed; hiding it makes it a habit.

## Recording and privacy

If students record you, follow campus policy. Do not record students without a rule. Do not post a student’s broken demo to social media, even as a joke.

## Romantic or sexual interest

If you feel it, you do not act on it. Transfer supervision if you must. If a student expresses interest, refuse clearly, document, tell the chair if needed. This is not optional professionalism.

## Illness and your absence

Have a backup: a recorded demo, a TA-led lab, a reading + quiz. Do not teach with a fever to prove dedication. The room will remember the quality, not the martyrdom.

## Exercise

Write four scripts on a card: dominate, slur, “you’re wrong,” public integrity. Practice them once out loud. Awkward in your kitchen is better than awkward in front of 40 people.
